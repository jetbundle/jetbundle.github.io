chapter 4 — multivariable geometry and high-dimensional probability

probability as volume; concentration as thermodynamic determinism

4.1 multiple integrals

over a rectangle, Riemann sums $\sum f(x_i,y_j)\Delta x\Delta y$ define $\iint f\,dx\,dy$. Fubini–type theorems justify iterated integrals when hypotheses hold.

4.2 Jacobians

for a $C^1$ change $(x,y)=\Phi(u,v)$, area (volume) elements transform with $dx\,dy=|\det J|\,du\,dv$ where $J$ is the Jacobian matrix of partial derivatives. this is the multivariable generalization of ch.2’s one-dimensional $|dx/du|$.

4.3 probability as measure (essentials)

a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$ assigns $P(A)\in[0,1]$ to measurable events, countably additive on disjoint unions, with $P(\Omega)=1$. when a density $f\ge 0$ exists, $P(A)=\int_A f\,d\mu$ and $\int_\Omega f\,d\mu=1$. the CDF $F(t)=P(X\le t)$ links to PDFs via $F'=f$ where differentiable.

4.4 canonical distributions

exponential: $f(x)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$ on $x\ge 0$ — memoryless waiting times. Gaussian: $$f(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\Big(-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big).$$ maxwell–boltzmann velocity components are Gaussian once kinetic energy enters $e^{-\beta p^2/(2m)}$.

4.5 LLN and CLT (operational statements)

sample means of i.i.d. variables with finite mean concentrate on $\mu$ (law of large numbers). centered sums scaled by $\sqrt{n}$ approach a normal distribution under finite-variance conditions (CLT). for thermodynamics, extensive observables have relative fluctuations $\sim n^{-1/2}$, vanishing as $n\to\infty$.

4.6 concentration

Chebyshev: $P(|X-\mu|\ge k\sigma)\le 1/k^2$. in high dimension, “typical sets” carry most of the measure; log-volume codes entropy.

4.7 bridge (verbal)

multiple integrals $\leftrightarrow$ phase-space volumes; Jacobians $\leftrightarrow$ canonical transformations; gaussians $\leftrightarrow$ quadratic hamiltonians after diagonalization; CLT + concentration $\leftrightarrow$ sharp macroscopic variables.

4.8 worked problems (condensed)

maxwell $v_x$ factor. $f(v_x)\propto e^{-mv_x^2/(2k_{\mathrm{B}}T)}$. gaussian normalization gives prefactor $\sqrt{m/(2\pi k_{\mathrm{B}}T)}$.

multivariate gaussian integral. for $A\succ 0$ symmetric, $$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} e^{-\frac12 x^{\mathsf T}Ax}\,d^nx=(2\pi)^{n/2}(\det A)^{-1/2}.$$ orthogonal diagonalization separates the integral into $n$ one-dimensional gaussians.
