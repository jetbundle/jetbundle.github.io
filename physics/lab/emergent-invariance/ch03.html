chapter 3 — linear structure and spectral invariance

state spaces as vector spaces; equilibrium as an eigenproblem

3.1 vector spaces

a vector space $V$ supports addition and scalar multiplication satisfying the usual axioms (associativity, commutativity, distributivity, existence of $0$ and additive inverses, $1\cdot v=v$). probability measures on a finite outcome set live in a simplex inside a finite-dimensional real vector space; infinite-dimensional analogues host fields of states in quantum theory.

a basis $\{e_i\}$ expresses each $v\in V$ uniquely as $v=\sum_i c_i e_i$.

3.2 linear operators

$A:V\to V$ is linear if $A(cu+dv)=cAu+dAv$. in finite dimensions choose a basis and represent $A$ as a matrix.

3.3 eigenvalues — invariant directions

solve $Av=\lambda v$ with $v\neq 0$. for a markov transition operator $P$, a stationary distribution satisfies $P\rho=\rho$ — an eigenvector with eigenvalue $1$.

3.4 inner products and diagonalization toolkit

an inner product $\langle u,v\rangle$ defines lengths $\|v\|=\sqrt{\langle v,v\rangle}$ and orthogonality. Gram–Schmidt orthogonalizes a linearly independent list. the adjoint $A^\dagger$ satisfies $\langle Au,v\rangle=\langle u,A^\dagger v\rangle$; for real matrices $A^\dagger=A^{\mathsf T}$.

3.5 spectral theorem (symmetric / Hermitian)

if $A=A^\dagger$, there exists an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors with real eigenvalues. observables in quantum mechanics are modeled as self-adjoint operators precisely so spectra are real.

3.6 quadratic forms

$Q(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Ax$ with symmetric $A$. orthogonal diagonalization writes $Q=\sum_i \lambda_i y_i^2$. small oscillations near equilibrium expand the potential to quadratic order — normal modes are eigenvectors of the stiffness matrix.

3.7 Markov operators (sketch)

stochastic matrices with positive irreducible structure admit a unique stationary distribution (Perron– Frobenius); the eigenvalue $1$ is simple and the corresponding eigenvector can be chosen strictly positive then normalized to a probability vector.

3.8 worked problems (condensed)

two coupled identical springs. potential $V=\tfrac12 k(x_1^2+x_2^2)+\tfrac12 k_c(x_1-x_2)^2$ leads to stiffness $K=\begin{pmatrix}k+k_c & -k_c\\ -k_c & k+k_c\end{pmatrix}$ with eigenvalues $k$ and $k+2k_c$ and modes $(1,1)$ / $(1,-1)$ — symmetric and antisymmetric combinations decouple the dynamics.

two-state markov chain. with $P=\begin{pmatrix}1-a & b\\ a & 1-b\end{pmatrix}$ and $a,b>0$, stationarity gives $b\rho_2=a\rho_1$, hence $\rho_1=b/(a+b)$, $\rho_2=a/(a+b)$.

Hermitian spectra are real. if $Av=\lambda v$ with $A=A^\dagger$, then $\lambda\langle v,v\rangle=\langle Av,v\rangle=\langle v,Av\rangle=\overline{\lambda}\langle v,v\rangle$, so $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$.
