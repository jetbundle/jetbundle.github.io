chapter 5 — local-to-global structure

topology and groupoids: gluing admissible local moves into global phases

5.1 topological spaces

a topological space is a set $X$ with a distinguished family of open subsets: $\emptyset,X$ are open; any union of opens is open; finite intersections of opens are open. continuity means preimages of opens are open. connectedness forbids splitting $X$ into two disjoint nonempty clopens; compactness asks that every open cover admits a finite subcover (heine–borel: closed bounded sets in $\mathbb{R}^n$).

5.2 manifolds

locally modeled on $\mathbb{R}^n$ with consistent transition maps between charts. configuration spaces of rigid bodies, circles of angles, and projective spaces of lines are standard examples.

5.3 categories and groupoids

a category has objects, morphisms, associative composition, and identities. a groupoid is a category where every morphism is invertible — partial symmetries with composability only when endpoints match. the fundamental groupoid $\Pi(X)$ has points as objects and homotopy classes of paths as morphisms; for nice spaces loops recover $\pi_1(X,x_0)$.

5.4 van Kampen (groupoid viewpoint)

when $X=U\cup V$ with $U,V$ open, global connectivity data is assembled from local groupoids on $U$, $V$, and $U\cap V$ via a pushout — a precise “local rules $\to$ global structure” machine.

5.5 group actions and quotients

a group $G$ acting on $X$ produces orbits $G\cdot x$ and the orbit space $X/G$. gauge redundancies and indistinguishable particles are modeled by quotienting a larger naive configuration space.

5.6 mapping to statistical physics (prose)

  • allowed local rearrangements $\leftrightarrow$ morphisms in a groupoid picture of dynamics
  • global equilibrium sectors $\leftrightarrow$ connected components / ergodic components
  • defect sectors (vortices, dislocations) $\leftrightarrow$ nontrivial homotopy classes
  • spontaneous symmetry breaking $\leftrightarrow$ degenerate ground-state manifolds and reduced effective descriptions on quotients

5.7 worked problems (condensed)

$\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb{Z}$. homotopy classes of loops are labeled by winding number; composition adds windings — integer vortex charge in planar XY-type stories.

periodic boundary conditions. identify $0\sim L$ on $[0,L]$ to obtain a circle: a quotient topology enforcing translation identification.

two identical particles on $\mathbb{R}$. swap symmetry identifies $(x_1,x_2)$ with $(x_2,x_1)$; the reduced configuration space is a half-plane with boundary conventions depending on bosonic vs fermionic statistics (phase factors on exchange paths in quantum lifts).
