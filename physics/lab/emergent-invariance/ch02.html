chapter 2 — continuous aggregation

the integral as the macroscopic limit of impossible discrete sums

2.1 why discrete sums fail at large $N$

for $N\sim 10^{23}$ particles the number of contributing microstates to a sum like $Z=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}$ is not enumerable on a computer or a chalkboard. the replacement is a coarse-grained integral over densities of states in energy or phase space. the mathematical content is: refine partitions until Riemann sums stabilize on a limit.

2.2 partitions, step functions, Darboux sums

on $[a,b]$, choose $a=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_n=b$ with mesh widths $\Delta x_k=x_k-x_{k-1}$. a step function is constant on each open subinterval; its integral is exactly $\sum_k c_k\Delta x_k$. for bounded $f$, lower/upper Darboux sums bracket the area; when refinements squeeze these together, $f$ is Riemann integrable and $$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=\lim_{\mathrm{mesh}\to 0}\sum_k f(\xi_k)\Delta x_k$$ for tag choices $\xi_k\in[x_{k-1},x_k]$.

2.3 density of states and $Z$

if energy levels lie densely with density $g(E)$, then $$Z(\beta)=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}\approx \int_0^\infty g(E)\,e^{-\beta E}\,dE.$$ the right-hand side is the continuum limit of a Riemann sum in energy bins.

2.4 fundamental theorem of calculus

if $F'=f$ with $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$, then $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b)-F(a)$. proof strategy: mean value theorem on each subinterval produces a telescoping Riemann sum for $F(b)-F(a)$.

2.5 change of variables (Jacobian preview)

for monotone $C^1$ substitutions $x=\phi(u)$, $$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=\int_{\phi^{-1}(a)}^{\phi^{-1}(b)} f(\phi(u))\,|\phi'(u)|\,du.$$ in higher dimensions $| \det J |$ scales volume elements — mandatory for phase-space integrals.

2.6 improper integrals and nascent $\delta$

define $\int_0^\infty f:=\lim_{b\to\infty}\int_0^b f$ when the limit exists. rectangular pulses of width $\varepsilon$ and height $1/\varepsilon$ approximate $\delta(x)$ in the sense that $\int f(x)\delta(x)\,dx=f(0)$ in the limit.

2.8 worked problems (condensed)

harmonic oscillator (1d classical). $H=p^2/(2m)+\frac12 kx^2$. up to the usual $h$-normalization, $$Z=\frac{1}{h}\iint e^{-\beta H}\,dp\,dx =\frac{1}{h}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi m}{\beta}}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\beta k}} =\frac{2\pi}{h\beta}\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}=\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{\hbar\omega}$$ with $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}$ in the standard identification.

change of variables for densities. for monotone $g$, conservation of probability gives $f_Y(y)=f_X(x)\,|dx/dy|$ — the same Jacobian logic as in phase-space coordinate changes.
