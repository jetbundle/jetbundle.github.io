1.1 why linearity is unavoidable

any smooth natural process looks linear when examined in a sufficiently small neighborhood. a curve is approximately a straight line near any point; a surface approximates a tangent plane. in quantum mechanics, states combine by linear superposition: if $|\psi\rangle$ and $|\phi\rangle$ are possible states, then $a|\psi\rangle + b|\phi\rangle$ is also admissible for complex amplitudes $a,b$. the mathematics is therefore forced to begin with linear spaces.

we develop the same object in three projections — computational (matrices), structural (morphisms, dual pairings), and geometric (tangent vectors, differentials) — and show they are inseparable.

1.2 linear spaces and bases (computational foundation)

definition. a linear (vector) space $V$ over a field $K$ (usually $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$) is a set with addition $u+v\in V$ and scalar multiplication $\lambda u\in V$ satisfying the usual axioms (commutativity, associativity, zero, negatives, distributivity).

vectors $\{e_1,\dots,e_n\}$ form a basis if they are linearly independent and span $V$. every $v\in V$ has unique components $(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ with $v=\sum_i x_i e_i$; changing bases changes components, not $V$ itself.

worked demonstration (gaussian elimination). consider $Ax=b$ with $$A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\2&4&6\\3&6&9\end{pmatrix},\qquad b=\begin{pmatrix}6\\12\\18\end{pmatrix}.$$ augment and eliminate: row $2\leftarrow$ row $2-2\cdot$row $1$, row $3\leftarrow$ row $3-3\cdot$row $1$ yield two zero rows; rank$(A)=1$ and the system is consistent. free variables $x_2=s$, $x_3=t$ and row $1$ give $x_1+2s+3t=6$, hence $$x=\begin{pmatrix}6\\0\\0\end{pmatrix} +s\begin{pmatrix}-2\\1\\0\end{pmatrix} +t\begin{pmatrix}-3\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}.$$ the solution affine space has dimension $3-\mathrm{rank}(A)=2$ (nullity). cramer’s rule applies when $\det A\neq 0$; here it is illustrative only.

1.3 linear operators and matrices

a linear operator $T:V\to V$ satisfies $T(u+v)=T(u)+T(v)$ and $T(\lambda u)=\lambda T(u)$. fixing a basis $\{e_j\}$, write $T(e_j)=\sum_i a_{ij} e_i$; the matrix $A=(a_{ij})$ represents $T$.

basis transition (conjugation). if new basis vectors satisfy $e_k'=\sum_j c_{jk} e_j$ with invertible $C=(c_{jk})$, then the matrix of $T$ transforms as $$A' = C^{-1} A C.$$ eigenvalues, trace, determinant, and the characteristic polynomial are invariant under conjugation.

1.4 dual space and evaluation pairing (structural view)

the dual space $V^\ast$ consists of linear functionals $f:V\to K$. given a basis $\{e_i\}$ of $V$, the dual basis $\{f^j\}$ satisfies $f^j(e_i)=\delta^j_i$. the evaluation pairing $\langle f,v\rangle := f(v)$ is bilinear and non-degenerate; $T$ induces $T^\ast$ via $\langle T^\ast f,v\rangle=\langle f,Tv\rangle$, represented by the transpose in dual coordinates.

1.5 geometric projection: tangent spaces and differentials

on a smooth manifold $M$, each point $p$ carries a tangent space $T_p M$ — the linear approximation of $M$ near $p$. a smooth map $F:M\to N$ has differential $dF_p:T_p M\to T_{F(p)}N$, the best linear approximation to $F$ at $p$.

coordinate change on $\mathbb{R}^n$. if $y=y(x)$, components of a tangent vector transform by $w^j=\sum_i (\partial y^j/\partial x^i)\, v^i$. the jacobian matrix $J=(\partial y^j/\partial x^i)$ implements the same linear law as the basis-change matrix $C$ above; iterated changes compose functorially, preserving tensorial meaning.

1.6 the full cycle — one object, three projections

  • computational: matrices, elimination, invariants read off entries.
  • structural: morphisms, dual pairings, functoriality across bases.
  • geometric: differentials $dF_p$, frame transitions, tensor transformation laws.

master problems for mastery

work in order; solutions should translate claims across the three projections where possible.

  1. solve $Ax=b$ for a $4\times 4$ system of rank $2$; report nullity and the general solution.
  2. given two bases related by $C$, conjugate a concrete rotation matrix and verify trace and determinant are unchanged.
  3. compute the jacobian for polar $\leftrightarrow$ cartesian coordinates; relate $|\det J|$ to the area element.
  4. prove that invertibility of $T$ is equivalent to $\det(A)\neq 0$, and interpret $|\det(A)|$ as volume scaling (preview of chapter 2).
